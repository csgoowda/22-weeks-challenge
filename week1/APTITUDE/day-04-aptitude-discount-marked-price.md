# Day 4 – Aptitude Notes
## Topic: Discount and Marked Price

---

# 1. Important Terms

### 1. Marked Price (MP)

Marked Price is the original price displayed on a product before giving any discount.

Example:

A shirt has a price tag of ₹1,000.

Marked Price = ₹1,000

### 2. Selling Price (SP)

Selling Price is the actual price paid by the customer after discount.

Example:

Marked Price = ₹1,000  
Discount = ₹200

Selling Price = ₹800

### 3. Discount

Discount is the amount reduced from the marked price.

Example:

Marked Price = ₹1,000  
Selling Price = ₹800

Discount = ₹200

---

# 2. Important Formulas

### Formula 1: Discount Amount

Discount = (Marked Price × Discount Percentage) / 100

### Formula 2: Selling Price

Selling Price = Marked Price - Discount

### Formula 3: Discount Percentage

Discount Percentage = (Discount / Marked Price) × 100

### Formula 4: Marked Price

Marked Price = Selling Price + Discount

---

# 3. Solved Examples

## Example 1: Find Discount Amount

Question:

A mobile phone has a marked price of ₹20,000. The shop offers a 10% discount. Find the discount amount.

Given:

MP = ₹20,000  
Discount = 10%

Solution:

Discount = (MP × Discount %) / 100

Discount = (20,000 × 10) / 100

Discount = ₹2,000

Answer: ₹2,000

---

## Example 2: Find Selling Price

Question:

A product has a marked price of ₹5,000 and a discount of 20%. Find its selling price.

Given:

MP = ₹5,000  
Discount = 20%

Step 1: Find discount amount.

Discount = (5,000 × 20) / 100

Discount = ₹1,000

Step 2: Find selling price.

SP = MP - Discount

SP = 5,000 - 1,000

Answer: ₹4,000

---

## Example 3: Find Discount Percentage

Question:

A product has a marked price of ₹800 and a selling price of ₹600. Find the discount percentage.

Given:

MP = ₹800  
SP = ₹600

Step 1: Find discount.

Discount = MP - SP

Discount = 800 - 600

Discount = ₹200

Step 2: Find discount percentage.

Discount % = (Discount / MP) × 100

= (200 / 800) × 100

= 25%

Answer: 25%

---

## Example 4: Find Marked Price

Question:

A product is sold for ₹900 after giving a discount of ₹100. Find its marked price.

Given:

SP = ₹900  
Discount = ₹100

Formula:

MP = SP + Discount

MP = 900 + 100

Answer: ₹1,000

---

## Example 5: Find Selling Price with 30% Discount

Question:

A product has a marked price of ₹1,500. The shop gives a 30% discount. Find the selling price.

Given:

MP = ₹1,500  
Discount = 30%

Customer pays = 100% - 30%

= 70%

SP = 1,500 × 70 / 100

SP = ₹1,050

Answer: ₹1,050

# 4. Quick Calculation Shortcuts

Instead of calculating the discount amount first, calculate the percentage the customer must pay.

| Discount | Customer Pays |
|---|---|
| 5% | 95% of MP |
| 10% | 90% of MP |
| 15% | 85% of MP |
| 20% | 80% of MP |
| 25% | 75% of MP |
| 30% | 70% of MP |
| 40% | 60% of MP |
| 50% | 50% of MP |
| 60% | 40% of MP |
| 75% | 25% of MP |

### Shortcut Example 1

MP = ₹1,500  
Discount = 20%

Customer pays = 80%

SP = 1,500 × 80 / 100

SP = ₹1,200

### Shortcut Example 2

MP = ₹2,000  
Discount = 25%

Customer pays = 75%

SP = 2,000 × 75 / 100

SP = ₹1,500

# 5. Important Percentage Shortcuts

Remember these calculations:

| Percentage | Fraction |
|---|---|
| 10% | 1/10 |
| 20% | 1/5 |
| 25% | 1/4 |
| 50% | 1/2 |
| 75% | 3/4 |

### Example

Find 25% of ₹800.

25% = 1/4

800 / 4 = ₹200

Therefore, 25% of ₹800 = ₹200.

---

# 6. Important Points to Remember

1. Marked Price is the original displayed price.
2. Selling Price is the actual price paid.
3. Discount is the reduction in price.
4. Discount is always calculated on Marked Price in these problems.
5. Selling Price = Marked Price - Discount.
6. Marked Price = Selling Price + Discount.
7. Customer Payment Percentage = 100% - Discount Percentage.
8. Do not confuse discount amount with discount percentage.
